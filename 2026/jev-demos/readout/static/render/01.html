Drayforge replays your production database traffic against a candidate version of your schema, so you find the slow query before your users do.

 drayforge replay --against migration/0194_partition_shipments.sql
  replayed  1,204,551 statements   window 60m   plan diff v4.12

  ▲ +1,893 ms  SELECT * FROM shipments WHERE carrier_ref = $1
             seq scan across 41 partitions   (was: idx_shipments_carrier)
  ▲   +41 ms  UPDATE shipments SET status = $2 WHERE id = $1
  ▼   -12 ms  SELECT count(*) FROM shipments WHERE created_at > $1
      ok       1,204,548 statements unchanged

You point Drayforge at a read replica. It records a sampling window of live queries — parameters, plan shapes, timing, lock waits — and then runs that recorded workload against a second instance carrying your pending migration. You get a diff: which statements got slower, by how much, and which index the planner stopped using. It works with PostgreSQL 13 through 18, MySQL 8.0 and 8.4, and MariaDB 11.4.

Capture adds 0.4 ms of median overhead at 10,000 queries per second, measured on a c7g.4xlarge. Replay of a one-hour window takes about nine minutes on default settings. The current release is 4.12, shipped August 2026. We have been running Drayforge in production for customers since March 2019, and the hosted service has held 99.97% uptime over the last twelve months, which you can check yourself on our public status page — we publish the raw incident log, including the 51-minute outage in February 2026 caused by our own certificate rotation.

What customers found

Harrowgate Freight moved a 900 GB shipments table to a partitioned layout. Drayforge caught that the partition key excluded a common lookup by carrier reference, which would have turned a 3 ms query into a 1.9-second sequential scan across 41 partitions. They fixed it before the migration ran.
Pellow Health runs Drayforge on every pull request that touches a migration. Since adopting it in 2021 they have had zero schema-related production incidents, down from an average of five a year.
Kettleborn Retail cut checkout p99 latency from 820 ms to 190 ms after Drayforge showed a composite index was being ignored because of a type mismatch on a bigint column.
Soren Analytics reduced its RDS bill by $18,000 a month by using replay to confirm it could drop 14 unused indexes without regressing any recorded query.
