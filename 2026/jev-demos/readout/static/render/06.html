Thornsalt sits between your job queue and your workers as a recording proxy. Every job execution is captured as a durable trace: the enqueued payload, every outbound HTTP and database call the worker made, the responses it got back, the timing, and the final outcome. When a job fails in production, you can pull that trace onto your laptop and re-run the job against the recorded responses. The worker code runs for real. The outside world is played back from tape. You can edit the code, re-run, and watch the same failure either reproduce or stop reproducing.

Supported today: Sidekiq (Ruby), Celery (Python), and River (Go). Traces are stored in your own S3-compatible bucket. We hold the index, not the payloads.

Status

What works today
  • Recording and replay for the three runtimes above.
  • Deterministic replay of HTTP and Postgres.
  • Trace retention and search by job class, queue, error, and time range.
  • A CLI that pulls a trace by ID and runs it locally.
  • Redaction rules for fields you do not want leaving your infrastructure, applied before anything is written.
What does not work yet
  • Replay is not deterministic for jobs that read the system clock in a way that changes control flow; we record the clock but do not yet patch it.
  • MySQL is recorded but not replayable.
  • Jobs that spawn subprocesses are recorded only at the boundary, so the subprocess itself is a black box.
  • There is no web UI for browsing traces; everything is CLI and API.
  • Retention is capped at 30 days.
  • We have not run a formal security audit, though we have written up our threat model and will send it to you.

Design partner
